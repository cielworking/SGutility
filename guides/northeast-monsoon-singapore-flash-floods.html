Northeast Monsoon Singapore: Flash Floods and PUB Alerts

What the Northeast Monsoon is, the latest MSS outlook, monsoon surges, and how to use official PUB flood alerts — without inventing hotspot lists or rainfall totals.

Updated 6 November 2026
WhatsApp

From late November into the early months of the year, Singapore’s weather often shifts from inter-monsoon thunderstorms toward the Northeast Monsoon. That is also when residents ask about flash floods, PUB alerts, and whether a Causeway trip is wise in heavy rain. This guide sticks to Meteorological Service Singapore (MSS), PUB, and related official advice — and does not invent rainfall totals, hotspot lists, or “best hours” to drive.

What the Northeast Monsoon is (usual timing)

MSS’s climate overview states that Singapore’s climate has two monsoon seasons separated by inter-monsoon periods. The Northeast Monsoon occurs from December to early March, with prevailing northerly to northeasterly winds. Source: MSS — Climate of Singapore.

MSS splits that season into:

  • Early / wet phase (usually December to early January): monsoon surges can bring widespread continuous moderate to heavy rain; afternoon and early evening showers also develop quickly.
  • Late / dry phase (usually late January to early March): typically windier and relatively drier than the wet phase.

The same page notes that higher rainfall months tend to fall from November to January during the wet phase, while February is often among the drier months in the dry phase — use that as climatology context, not as a forecast for any single week.

Current MSS monsoon outlook (cited with date)

MSS’s Monsoon Update page, updated on 29 October 2026, stated that the region was still in the inter-monsoon period (winds mostly light and variable), with thundery showers expected on most days in the coming weeks. For November 2026, MSS expected near-normal rainfall and above-normal temperatures over Singapore. It also said the Northeast Monsoon is expected to progressively set in towards the end of November 2026 or early December 2026, with winds strengthening and blowing mainly from the northwest or northeast. Source: MSS — Monsoon Update (check the page for newer updates after 29 Oct 2026).

For day-to-day planning, pair that seasonal note with live tools on SG Alerts: rain radar, Singapore rain forecast, and Malaysia rain forecast.

Monsoon surges

MSS describes a Northeast Monsoon surge as a sudden southward surge of cold air from continental northern Asia into the South China Sea during northern-hemisphere winter (December–March). As those winds warm and pick up moisture over the sea, dense rain clouds can form near the equator. When winds are particularly strong, coastal areas including Singapore can see moderate to heavy showers or rain and occasional thunderstorms that may be continuous and prolonged for two to several days. Surges are more frequent and intense in the early part of the season (November–January) than later (February–March); dry-phase surges more often bring strong winds without heavy showers. Source: MSS — Weather Systems (Monsoon Surge).

MSS states that, on average, Singapore experiences about 2 to 4 monsoon surges each year, with each event lasting about 1 to 5 days of widespread continuous moderate to heavy rain. Treat those as climatological averages from MSS, not a prediction that a surge starts on a named calendar day.

Flash floods — and where PUB publishes hotspots

PUB explains that drains usually cope, but extremely heavy rainfall over a short period can exceed capacity. A flash flood is a small, localised flood that typically does not last more than an hour. PUB also notes that flood-prone areas have fallen sharply over decades through drainage investment, while climate change can still bring more intense storms. Source: PUB — About Floods.

Do not copy hotspot addresses from blogs. PUB publishes official lists — for example a flood-prone areas list (as of Nov 2025 on that page) and a hotspots list (as of Feb 2026 on that page). Always open the current PDFs/links from PUB’s About Floods page rather than relying on a screenshot in this guide.

How to get PUB / MSS flood and heavy-rain alerts

  • PUB Flood Alerts on Telegram — PUB’s flood safety materials point the public to the official @pubfloodalerts channel (also referenced from PUB’s flood-safety brochure / Get Flood-Wise pages).
  • myENV app — PUB’s flood-safety tips say to download myENV and enable alerts such as Heavy Rain, Drain Water Level, and Flash Flood Alerts. MSS also notes heavy-rain warnings via myENV. Sources: PUB — Flood Safety Tips; MSS FAQ.
  • SMS — PUB’s SMS subscription page lets you receive MSS heavy-rain warning SMS and progressive high-water-level alerts for selected canal/drain sensors at 50%, 75%, 90% and 100% of canal depth. Source: PUB — Subscribe to SMS Alerts.
  • X / other PUB channels — PUB’s flood-ready materials also mention following PUB’s official social updates (for example @PUBsingapore on X in the flood brochure). Prefer channels named on PUB.gov.sg.

Water-level SMS thresholds are relative to the sensor’s canal depth. A rising percentage means the monitored waterway is filling — use it with radar and local conditions, not as a standalone “island-wide flood” meter.

Driving or heading to JB in heavy rain (official advice only)

PUB’s motorist tips (Flood Safety Tips) say to:

  • Follow PUB flood alert channels and plan your route accordingly.
  • Avoid driving through a flooded area above road-kerb height or where road markings are no longer visible.
  • If a flooded stretch is unavoidable and assessed passable, drive slowly and steadily; avoid braking in a way that drops engine speed; stay toward the middle where water is often shallower; go one vehicle at a time.
  • If floodwaters rise and stall the car, do not restart; turn on hazards, call for assistance, and move to higher ground.
  • After exiting a flood, gently tap brakes to dry them and test before driving on.

Source: PUB — Flood Safety Tips (motorists).

For a Johor trip, combine that with live checkpoint cameras and clearance prep — not with invented “leave at X pm” charts:

  • Woodlands cameras / Tuas cameras and Woodlands vs Tuas
  • Year-end Causeway timing and MyICA QR
  • Fuel / VEP rules: JB petrol & VEP tips

If haze coincides with wet weather, check Singapore PSI / haze separately — rain and air quality are different dashboards.

A simple monsoon-season check routine

  1. Read the latest MSS monsoon / weather outlook (start at the Monsoon Update page).
  2. Open SG Alerts radar and rain-forecast pages before outdoor or cross-border plans.
  3. Ensure myENV / PUB Telegram / SMS alerts you need are enabled.
  4. If water covers a road above kerb height or hides markings, turn back — PUB’s wording, not a social-media dare.
  5. For JB, verify cameras and documents; delay the trip if flooding or extreme rain is active on your route.

Bottom line: The Northeast Monsoon’s usual window is December to early March (MSS), with a wetter early phase and possible monsoon surges. For this season’s onset timing, trust the dated MSS Monsoon Update — and for floods, follow PUB’s live alerts and official hotspot lists, not copied tables.
