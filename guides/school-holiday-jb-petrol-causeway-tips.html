School-Holiday JB Trips: Petrol, VEP and Causeway Tips

RON95 rules for Singapore cars in Malaysia, Singapore’s three-quarter tank rule, JPJ VEP RFID and RM20 Road Charge via Touch ’n Go, plus school-holiday crossing tips with live camera links.

Updated 6 November 2026
WhatsApp

School holidays are when many Singapore households drive into Johor. The calendar is clear; the rules that catch people out are usually fuel (both sides of the border), Malaysia’s Vehicle Entry Permit, and Touch ’n Go payments — not the cameras alone.

This guide covers petrol and crossing basics for Singapore-registered cars, using official Singapore Customs / ICA / LTA pages, Malaysian JPJ VEP materials, and Malaysian government or major news statements on RON95. It does not invent wait times.

School-holiday window

MOE’s academic calendar lists Term 4 school holidays (MK, Primary & Secondary) from Saturday, 21 November 2026 to Thursday, 31 December 2026. Source: MOE academic calendar. For Christmas / New Year public holidays and a longer Causeway planning note, see Year-end school holidays and Causeway timing.

ICA year-end advisory: as of writing, we have not found a dedicated ICA media-room advisory covering the full 21 Nov–31 Dec 2026 school holidays. Check ICA’s media room closer to your trip. Until one appears, use live cameras rather than guessed “peak hour” charts.

1. Malaysia RON95: Singapore cars cannot buy the subsidised grade

Malaysia’s Finance Ministry has stated publicly that foreign-registered vehicles (including Singapore) are not allowed to buy RON95 and should buy RON97, which is floated. Source: Ministry of Finance Malaysia — foreign vehicles and RON95 (Bernama / MOF release).

That foreign-vehicle restriction on subsidised RON95 has been in place for years under Malaysia’s Control of Supplies framework; Domestic Trade and Cost of Living Ministry (KPDN) enforcement at border-area stations is regularly reported. From 1 April 2026, CNA reported that Malaysia would strengthen rules so action can be taken against buyers of subsidised RON95 for foreign-registered vehicles as well as station operators, under the Control of Supplies Act 1961. Source: CNA — foreign vehicles and RON95 penalties.

Penalties: CNA’s reporting of court and ministry coverage describes maximums under that Act in the order of large fines and possible jail terms for convicted motorists (figures quoted in those articles). Treat those as news reports of the legal framework — always confirm current enforcement language with KPDN / official gazettes if you need a formal citation for court or compliance work. Practical rule for drivers: do not pump RON95 into a Singapore-registered car; use RON97 (or diesel where appropriate).

Before you leave Singapore, compare pump prices at home on Singapore petrol prices — the three-quarter tank rule (next section) means you should largely fill in Singapore anyway.

2. Singapore’s three-quarter tank rule

If you drive a Singapore-registered vehicle out via Woodlands or Tuas, Singapore Customs and ICA require the fuel tank to be at least three-quarters full. The rule covers petrol, diesel and CNG; for petrol+CNG hybrids, both tanks must meet three-quarters. Sources: Singapore Customs — Three-Quarter Tank Rule; ICA — Driving in and out of Singapore; LTA OneMotoring — Vehicles registered in Singapore.

Customs states that non-compliance may bring a composition sum of up to S$500, or prosecution in court, plus a required U-turn at the checkpoint. Tampering with fuel gauges can attract separate charges. Fill before you join the checkpoint queue.

3. Malaysia VEP (Vehicle Entry Permit) and RFID tag

Malaysia’s Road Transport Department (JPJ) requires foreign-registered vehicles to obtain a VEP RFID tag via its portals. JPJ’s English VEP RFID FAQ states:

  • Register at https://vep.jpj.gov.my, then complete payment and collection options at https://vepams.jpj.gov.my.
  • Tag processing fee: RM10; collection agent service fee: RM29.50; courier fee subject to the courier provider.
  • Activate by validating the tag serial, fitting the tag as shown in JPJ’s guide, uploading a fitment photo, and waiting for activation notice.
  • Validity: 5 years from the day the tag is activated.
  • The tag is unique to one vehicle and cannot be transferred.
  • A Touch ’n Go RFID tag is not a substitute for the VEP RFID tag.

Source: JPJ — VEP RFID TAG FAQ. Always re-check the live JPJ portals before you travel; fees and collection options can change.

Enforcement status: JPJ’s VEP programme pages describe recording of foreign vehicle entry and linkage to Road Charge collection. Malaysian news outlets have reported staged advocacy and later stricter compounding for vehicles without a valid activated VEP. This guide does not invent a single “fine amount” beyond what JPJ’s own FAQ pages state for tag fees. For current roadside penalties, read JPJ notices on vep.jpj.gov.my before you go.

4. Touch ’n Go and Road Charge (RC)

JPJ’s Road Charge FAQ states that foreign private vehicles entering Malaysia pay a Road Charge of RM20 per entry. Motorcycles, commercial vehicles, and certain government/embassy vehicles are exempt as listed in that FAQ. Payment is via Touch ’n Go card or Touch ’n Go eWallet — not cash. Road Charge is separate from highway tolls. Source: JPJ — FAQ RC (also summarised on JPJ-linked explainers such as Road Charge overview).

Top up in ringgit before you rely on the card at the border. LTA separately reminds Singapore drivers to carry enough value on a Singapore stored-value card for Singapore checkpoint tolls when exiting/re-entering — see the OneMotoring page linked above for Singapore-side toll tables.

5. When to cross (without inventing peaks)

  1. Confirm school-holiday and public-holiday dates (MOE / MOM).
  2. Check whether ICA has posted a fresh land-checkpoint advisory.
  3. Before you leave, open Woodlands cameras and Tuas cameras and read the timestamps.
  4. Have MyICA QR codes ready — see MyICA QR clearance.
  5. Verify three-quarter tank, VEP activation, and Touch ’n Go balance before you join the queue.

Bottom line: Fill to ≥¾ tank in Singapore, never buy Malaysian RON95 in a Singapore car, carry an activated JPJ VEP RFID tag with Touch ’n Go ready for the RM20 Road Charge, then choose Woodlands or Tuas with live cameras — not rumour.
