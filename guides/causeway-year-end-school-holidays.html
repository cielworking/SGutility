Year-End School Holidays and Causeway Timing

MOE year-end school holidays 21 Nov–31 Dec 2026, Christmas and New Year public holidays, and how to check Woodlands and Tuas cameras before you cross — without guessing unannounced ICA peaks.

Updated 6 November 2026
WhatsApp

The stretch from late November into early January is when many Singapore households plan Johor trips, longer Malaysia drives, or visits home across the Causeway and Second Link. Congestion can be heavy — but the honest way to plan is to lock the calendar facts from MOE and MOM, watch for any ICA advisory when one is published, and check live cameras on the day you travel. This guide does not invent peak hours or wait times.

The dates that are confirmed

MOE year-end school holidays

According to the Ministry of Education academic calendar, Term 4 school holidays for MOE Kindergarten, Primary and Secondary run from Saturday, 21 November 2026 to Thursday, 31 December 2026. Source: MOE academic calendar.

MOE’s separate press release on 2027 school terms states that the 2027 school year for MOE primary and secondary schools starts on Monday, 4 January 2027. Source: School Terms and Holidays for 2027 (MOE). That means the bridge from New Year’s weekend into the new term is short — useful context if you are returning to Singapore for school reopen.

Public holidays inside / beside that window

From the Ministry of Manpower public holidays list:

  • Christmas Day — Friday, 25 December 2026
  • New Year’s Day — Friday, 1 January 2027

Sources: MOM — Public holidays; 2026 gazette notice Public Holidays in 2026 (eGazette); 2027 release Public Holidays for 2027 (MOM).

Because both Christmas 2026 and New Year’s Day 2027 fall on Fridays, each creates a Friday–Sunday block without needing annual leave. That is a calendar observation only — it is not a forecast of checkpoint volume.

Past context: Deepavali long weekend (6–9 November 2026)

ICA did publish a land-checkpoint advisory for the Deepavali long weekend. That advisory covered Friday, 6 November to Monday, 9 November 2026, expected heavy traffic at Woodlands and Tuas, and encouraged travellers to use non-peak hours such as early mornings and late evenings, to check traffic before leaving, and to use MyICA QR codes. It also cited high volumes during the September 2026 school holidays and longer waits for some car travellers during peaks. Source: ICA — Heavy Traffic Expected at Land Checkpoints during Upcoming Deepavali Long Weekend.

That Deepavali window is now past. Use it only as a reminder that ICA sometimes issues holiday-specific advisories with practical tips — not as a copy-paste prediction for Christmas or New Year.

Has ICA published a year-end 2026 advisory yet?

Not that we could find at the time of writing this guide. Unlike the Deepavali advisory linked above, we did not locate a matching ICA media-room advisory that specifically covers the 21 November–31 December 2026 school holidays, Christmas 2026, or New Year 2027 land-checkpoint peaks.

Until ICA publishes one, do not treat third-party “typical peak Friday” charts as official forecasts. Check ICA’s news and media room closer to your travel date, and rely on live cameras the day you go.

What to do instead of guessing peak hours

  1. Mark the school-holiday block (21 Nov–31 Dec 2026) and the two Friday public holidays (25 Dec 2026, 1 Jan 2027) on your family calendar.
  2. Decide Woodlands vs Tuas by geography and live conditions, not habit. Northern/central Singapore to central JB often favours Woodlands; western origins or western Johor destinations often favour Tuas — but only if cameras agree. See Woodlands vs Tuas.
  3. Check both camera pages before you leave: Woodlands traffic cameras and Tuas traffic cameras. Look at the timestamps; an old frame is not useful.
  4. Prepare MyICA QR codes early so you are not configuring the app in the queue. ICA’s QR rules for cars, motorcycles, lorries, and buses are summarised in our MyICA QR clearance guide and on ICA’s QR page.
  5. Bring passports even if you clear Singapore immigration by QR. ICA is clear that the passport remains required for travel.
  6. Watch for construction and diversions at both checkpoints when ICA or on-site marshals advise them — the Deepavali advisory, for example, noted ongoing works linked to Woodlands redevelopment and Tuas APCS implementation. Conditions change; follow signs on the day.

How to read the cameras without over-claiming

Camera images show Singapore-side approach and plaza conditions in the frame. They do not measure Malaysian immigration time, and they do not publish an official “minutes to clear” number. Use them as a go / wait / switch-checkpoint signal:

  • If Woodlands looks heavily queued and Tuas looks clearer (or the reverse), factor in your extra driving time across Singapore before you switch.
  • If both look heavy, delaying the trip or shifting to a less popular hour is often wiser than refreshing social media rumours.
  • Pair cameras with weather on the SG Alerts dashboard — wet roads can slow merging even when booths look free.

Documents and side tasks people forget

Year-end trips fail for paperwork as often as for queues. Before a school-holiday crossing, confirm the basics ICA and LTA already publish elsewhere: passport validity for overseas travel, MyICA QR readiness for eligible travellers, and — if you drive a foreign-registered vehicle into Singapore — a valid Vehicle Entry Permit (VEP) as LTA and ICA require. The Deepavali advisory also reminded short-term visitors about the enhanced SG Arrival Card via MyICA; citizens and PRs still benefit from having the app updated for QR clearance.

Fuel and Touch ’n Go / RFID arrangements on the Malaysian side are outside SG Alerts’ Singapore camera pages. Do those checks with the relevant Malaysian operators. On the Singapore side, stick to what you can verify the same morning: official holiday dates, any new ICA advisory in the media room, and the live Woodlands/Tuas frames with readable timestamps.

A calm year-end crossing checklist

  • School holidays: 21 Nov–31 Dec 2026 (MOE)
  • Christmas PH: Fri 25 Dec 2026 (MOM)
  • New Year PH: Fri 1 Jan 2027 (MOM); MOE schools resume 4 Jan 2027
  • ICA year-end advisory: check media room nearer the date — none verified for this guide yet
  • MyICA QR generated; passports packed
  • Woodlands and Tuas cameras checked with fresh timestamps

Bottom line: The calendar is fixed by MOE and MOM. Checkpoint load is not something this page will invent. When ICA publishes a year-end advisory, follow it; until then, prepare documents and QR codes, then decide with Woodlands and Tuas live cameras on the day.
