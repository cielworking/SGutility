Chinese New Year 2027: Causeway and JB Traffic

Official CNY 2027 dates, where to watch for an ICA or LTA advisory, and the existing SG Alerts guides for MyICA, cameras, and JB petrol/VEP rules. Waiting times and “best hours” stay TBC.

Updated 12 November 2026
WhatsApp

This page is for planning a Chinese New Year 2027 trip through Woodlands or Tuas to Johor Bahru. It sticks to official holiday dates and to live tools you can actually check. It does not invent waiting times, “best hours,” or a 2027 ICA peak calendar — those stay TBC until ICA or LTA publishes them.

The full 2027 public-holiday list and other long weekends live on Singapore public holidays 2027 and long weekends. Petrol, VEP and tank rules are already covered in School-holiday JB trips: petrol, VEP and Causeway tips — this page points there instead of repeating them. The year-end school holidays and Causeway guide is a different window (November 2026 through early January 2027).

Official CNY 2027 dates (what MOM actually says)

Two official MOM pages name the same gazetted Chinese New Year days, and they word Monday, 8 February 2027 slightly differently. Both are cited below. Do not flatten them into a single unofficial “6–8 Feb holiday” label without reading the wording that applies to your work week.

  • Saturday, 6 February 2027 and Sunday, 7 February 2027 are listed as Chinese New Year — two of the 11 gazetted public holidays for 2027. Source: MOM, Public Holidays for 2027 (18 June 2026); MOM, Public holidays.
  • The 18 June 2026 press release attaches footnote 1 to Sunday, 7 February, and states, verbatim: “Monday, 8 Feb 2027, will be a public holiday.”
  • MOM’s ongoing public-holidays page states, verbatim: “Monday, 8 February 2027, will be a public holiday if your rest day falls on 7 February 2027.”

That “if your rest day” clause matches MOM’s general Employment Act explanation: if a public holiday falls on your rest day, the following working day is a paid public holiday; if it falls on a non-working day (Saturday, for many people on a five-day week), you are entitled to another day off or an extra day’s salary in lieu. Source: MOM, Public holidays: entitlement and pay. Confirm your own rest-day and substitution arrangements with your employer. This guide will not decide them for you.

For schools, MOE’s 2027 terms release says Chinese New Year is Saturday 6 February and Sunday 7 February, that Monday, 8 February will be a public holiday, and that Tuesday, 9 February will be a designated day off-in-lieu for schools (schools, including the General Office, Student Care Centres and KCare Centres, will be closed). Source: MOE, School Terms and Holidays for 2027. That Tuesday is a school closure, not an additional gazetted public holiday on MOM’s list of 11.

Chinese New Year’s Eve and any half-day

The calendar day before the first gazetted CNY holiday is Friday, 5 February 2027. That date is not one of the 11 gazetted public holidays on MOM’s 2027 list.

A public-service or nationwide early-release / half-day on CNY eve is TBC. As of 12 November 2026, no Public Service Division circular announcing a 5 February 2027 early release was found. Do not treat eve as a public holiday, and do not assume a half-day, until an official PSD or employer notice says so. Watch PSD’s newsroom and your own HR note. If such a notice appears, this section can be updated with that URL — not with unofficial “usual practice” claims.

ICA and LTA advisories — TBC for 2027

As of 12 November 2026, no ICA or LTA advisory for Chinese New Year 2027 land-checkpoint traffic was found. Until one is published, this page will not invent a 2027 peak window, busiest day, or waiting time.

Past (labelled as 2026, not a 2027 forecast): ICA did publish a Lunar New Year 2026 notice, “Very Heavy Traffic Expected at The Land Checkpoints for The Upcoming Lunar New Year,” dated 6 February 2026. It covered Friday, 13 February to Monday, 23 February 2026, and pointed travellers to LTA OneMotoring traffic updates, ICA’s own channels, and MyICA QR clearance. Source: ICA media room, 6 February 2026. That window and any waiting times mentioned in it apply to 2026 only.

Watch, closer to February 2027:

  • ICA media room
  • LTA newsroom
  • OneMotoring traffic information

When a 2027 notice is out, replace this TBC block with the official window and ICA’s own tips, cite the new URL, and bump the “Updated” date.

What to do instead of guessing hours

  • Before you leave, open the live cameras on Woodlands Checkpoint and Tuas Checkpoint. Read the timestamps. Do not treat an old social-media queue photo as current.
  • If you are choosing a crossing, use Woodlands vs Tuas Checkpoint together with those cameras — not a fixed “Woodlands is always slower at CNY” rule. This page will not invent one.
  • Generate a MyICA QR before you reach the booth. Steps, who can use it, and what to still bring are in MyICA QR clearance at Woodlands and Tuas. Official page: ICA, QR Code for Immigration Clearance at Land Checkpoints.

Petrol, the three-quarter tank rule, VEP and RON95

These rules are not CNY-specific, and they already have a dedicated guide. Use that page rather than a second copy here:

  • Singapore’s three-quarter tank rule when departing by road — official: Singapore Customs, Three-Quarter Tank Rule.
  • Malaysia VEP / RFID, Touch ’n Go Road Charge, and the rule that Singapore-registered cars cannot buy subsidised RON95 — see School-holiday JB: petrol, VEP and Causeway tips, which cites Customs, LTA/OneMotoring, ICA, MOF Malaysia and JPJ.
  • For a Singapore board-price check before you fill up, use How to check petrol prices today and the petrol dashboard.

A short CNY crossing checklist

  1. Confirm your own public-holiday dates against MOM (and, if you have school-age children, MOE’s 8–9 February school note).
  2. Look for a 2027 ICA/LTA land-checkpoint advisory; if none is up yet, treat peaks as TBC.
  3. Prepare the MyICA QR and the documents ICA still requires.
  4. Recheck the three-quarter tank, VEP and RON95 rules on the JB tips guide (and the official pages it cites).
  5. Open Woodlands and Tuas cameras just before you leave; reread Woodlands vs Tuas if you have a choice of crossing.

Bottom line: MOM names Chinese New Year on 6 and 7 February 2027, and both MOM pages discuss Monday, 8 February 2027 as a public holiday, with the holidays page adding the rest-day condition quoted above. Eve (5 February) is not a gazetted public holiday; any half-day is TBC. A 2027 checkpoint advisory is also TBC. Use cameras, MyICA, and the existing JB-tips and Woodlands vs Tuas guides — not invented wait times.
