COE Cat A vs Cat B Explained: How Bidding Works and What Results Mean

Learn how LTA defines COE Category A and B, how open bidding and reserve prices work, and how to read quota premiums on OneMotoring.

Updated 6 November 2026
WhatsApp

If you are buying a car in Singapore, the Certificate of Entitlement (COE) is usually the largest upfront cost after the vehicle itself. Category A and Category B are the two car bands most private buyers care about. This guide explains how LTA defines them, how open bidding works, and how to read results — based on LTA OneMotoring’s COE page.

What a COE is

A COE gives you the right to register and use a vehicle in Singapore for 10 years. COEs are released under the Vehicle Quota System (VQS), which caps new vehicle registrations given limited road space. Open bidding exercises are held twice a month. Before each exercise, LTA announces the quota available in each category.

At the end of the 10-year period you can deregister the vehicle or renew by paying the Prevailing Quota Premium (PQP) — a moving average of recent COE prices for the category — without bidding again.

Cat A vs Cat B (current rules)

LTA updated car categorisation from the May 2022 first bidding exercise onwards:

Category A

  • Non-fully electric cars with engines up to 1,600cc and maximum power output up to 97kW (130bhp); and
  • Fully electric cars with maximum power output up to 110kW (147bhp).

Category B

  • Non-fully electric cars with engines above 1,600cc or maximum power output above 97kW (130bhp); and
  • Fully electric cars with maximum power output above 110kW (147bhp).

Power and engine thresholds both matter for non-EV cars: a small-capacity engine with high power can still fall into Cat B. Always confirm the classification for the exact model with your dealer or LTA materials before bidding. Note that LTA has also run public consultation on proposed future changes to car categorisation — check OneMotoring for the latest status rather than assuming today’s bands are permanent forever.

Other categories exist (C goods/bus, D motorcycle, E open except motorcycle) but are outside this Cat A vs Cat B focus. Cat A and Cat B temporary COEs obtained by individuals are not transferable.

How open bidding works

Exercises usually start at 12pm on the first and third Monday of the month and last three working days, typically ending 4pm Wednesday if there is no public holiday in between. Exact dates are published on OneMotoring (including the 2026 schedule PDF).

Reserve price: You enter the maximum you are willing to pay. The system raises your effective bid in $1 steps up toward that reserve. The Current COE Price (CCP) is conceptually the highest unsuccessful bid plus $1, constrained by quota. If CCP rises above your reserve, you are out unless you revise upward.

Uniform price: At close, all successful bidders in a category pay the same Quota Premium for that exercise — not their individual maximum. Bidding higher than the eventual premium does not make you pay more than winners who bid exactly at the clearing level; it only improves your chance of staying in.

Who can bid: Individuals (18+) may submit one bid per exercise in their own name/ID/bank account, typically via DBS/POSB ATM. Companies and motor traders can bid online via participating banks (DBS, UOB, Maybank) and may submit multiple bids. Banks charge administration fees; a bid deposit is required (LTA lists $10,000 for Categories A, B, C and E).

After a successful submission you receive an Acknowledgement Code used for revisions. Successful winners receive a Temporary COE (TCOE). For Cat A/B, LTA states the TCOE must be used to register a vehicle within six months or the full deposit can be forfeited.

How to read bidding results

  • Quota Premium (QP): The clearing price successful bidders pay for that category in that exercise.
  • Quota: How many COEs were available; unused quota can be carried forward under LTA’s rules.
  • PQP: Relevant when renewing an existing vehicle’s COE without bidding — average of recent premiums, not identical to the latest QP.
  • Cat A vs Cat B gap: Cat B premiums are often higher because the pool includes larger/more powerful cars, but the gap varies with demand and quota each exercise.

Most retail buyers never bid personally: dealers bid as part of a guaranteed or non-guaranteed COE package. Understand whether your package locks a price, and what happens if the bid fails.

Practical tips for Singapore buyers

  1. Confirm whether your intended car is Cat A or Cat B using LTA’s power/engine rules (and EV kW limits).
  2. Watch several exercises of results on SG Alerts latest COE prices before committing — one spike is not a trend.
  3. If bidding yourself, keep enough funds for deposit + bank fees, store the Acknowledgement Code securely, and monitor CCP during the exercise via OneMotoring or your bank channel.
  4. Plan registration timing so the TCOE does not expire.
  5. Treat dealer “guaranteed COE” fine print as part of the car price negotiation.

For authoritative detail, always prefer LTA COE Open Bidding over secondary summaries.

Cat E and why dealers talk about it

Category E is an open category (all except motorcycles). Dealers sometimes use Cat E strategically when Cat A or B premiums are extreme. Cat E COEs obtained by individuals can be transferred once under LTA rules; company-bid Cat E COEs cannot. For most private buyers shopping a specific family car, the practical question remains whether that model sits in Cat A or Cat B under the engine/power (or EV kW) tests.

Reading a results table without overreacting

When you open latest COE prices, look at the premium, the quota, and whether the exercise was the first or second of the month. A one-exercise spike after a holiday can reverse in the next round. Compare Cat A and Cat B together: if Cat B jumps while Cat A is flat, demand may be concentrating in larger cars rather than signalling a universal market panic.

Unsuccessful individual bidders generally see the deposit returned after bank fees, one working day after results (per LTA’s description). Successful bidders must track TCOE expiry closely. If you are financing a car, align loan timelines with TCOE validity so you are not forced into a last-minute registration scramble.

Policy watch

LTA has consulted publicly on possible future refinements to how cars are split between mass-market and higher-value bands. Until any change is confirmed and effective dates are published on OneMotoring, use the May 2022-defined Cat A/B rules above. Bookmark LTA’s COE pages rather than screenshots from chat groups.
