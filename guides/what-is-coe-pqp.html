What Is COE PQP? Prevailing Quota Premium Explained

PQP is the renewal premium — a moving average of recent quota premiums — not the same number as the latest bidding result.

Updated 6 November 2026

When people say “COE price” they often mean the quota premium from the latest bidding exercise. Renewing an existing car is a different bill: the Prevailing Quota Premium (PQP). This guide explains what PQP is, how LTA describes the calculation, and how to look up the figure that applies to your renewal — without treating the latest Cat A/B result as your renewal price.

PQP vs the COE you see after each bidding

Twice a month, open bidding sets a Quota Premium (QP) for each category. Those results are what most dashboards (including SG Alerts latest COE) highlight. Source context: OneMotoring — COE Open Bidding.

At the end of a vehicle’s COE period, OneMotoring’s renewal page says you can deregister or renew by paying the PQP for your vehicle category. You do not re-bid for a renewal COE in the open bidding exercise. Source: OneMotoring — COE Renewal.

How LTA defines PQP

OneMotoring states that the PQP is the moving average of the Quota Premiums (QPs) in the last 3 months where bidding exercises were conducted. It varies monthly depending on those QPs. Months with no bidding are excluded from the calculation. You can find out the PQP for each month once the 2nd bidding exercise in the previous month is over. Source: OneMotoring COE Renewal page.

That is why PQP can sit above or below the single latest QP you saw on a results page. A quiet month of premiums pulls the average one way; a spike month pulls it another. For the arithmetic LTA used in a historical no-bidding period (2020), OneMotoring documents the exclusion rule on the same page — use that as methodology, not as today’s dollar figure.

5-year vs 10-year renewal (amounts you must look up)

OneMotoring lets you renew for 5 years or 10 years, subject to vehicle category and statutory lifespan rules:

  • 10-year renewal: pay the PQP for your category.
  • 5-year renewal: pay 50% of the PQP, rounded up to the nearest dollar.

Category-specific limits also apply. For Categories A, B and D, OneMotoring says you can renew for 5 years only once; after that 5-year period you cannot renew again and must deregister. Category C has different ongoing 5-year rules tied to statutory lifespan. Category E vehicles renew under the corresponding A–D category. Source: OneMotoring COE Renewal — read the category sections before you decide.

Do not use any dollar amount from this guide. PQP changes monthly. Check LTA’s published PQP (OneMotoring links an enquire / latest PQP path from the renewal page) for the month that applies to your chosen renewal date.

Which month’s PQP you pay

OneMotoring explains that the PQP rate depends on when you renew relative to your COE expiry date:

  • Renew early (before expiry month rules on the page): your new COE period can start from the 1st day of the month after you renew, and you forfeit any unused portion of the previous COE (including lay-up rebate effects described on the page).
  • Renew in the month your COE expires: the new period starts the day after the previous COE expires, and you do not forfeit the unused portion.
  • Late renewal: after expiry the vehicle is automatically deregistered until renewed; you pay the PQP of the expiry month plus a late renewal fee, and you must renew within 1 month or dispose of the vehicle. Late fee amounts by vehicle type are tabulated on OneMotoring — read the live table, do not rely on memory.

OneMotoring walks through dated examples (including an August 2017 expiry worked example) so you can map the Friday-after-2nd-bidding timing. Use those examples as a pattern, then plug in your real expiry date on the official page. Source: OneMotoring COE Renewal.

Practical renewal checklist

  1. Confirm vehicle category and whether Category E history means you renew under A–D (OneMotoring).
  2. Note COE expiry date from your documents / OneMotoring vehicle enquiry.
  3. Decide early vs expiry-month renewal after reading forfeiture rules.
  4. Look up the official PQP for the applicable month and category; for 5-year, apply 50% rounded up as LTA states.
  5. Check statutory lifespan (especially goods vehicles, buses, taxis, some motorcycles) before paying.
  6. Renew online on OneMotoring within the hours they publish (they note online payment is unavailable midnight–6am).

Why PQP shows up in chat groups after every 2nd bidding

Once the second bidding exercise of a month ends, OneMotoring says the next month’s PQP can be published. That timing is why renewal conversations spike on results night even when nobody in the chat is bidding. The QP just printed is an input to a future average; the payable renewal figure is the published PQP for the month that matches your renewal choice. Source: OneMotoring — COE Renewal.

If you are comparing “renew vs replace”, remember replacement means winning a new COE at the next QP (plus ARF and other registration costs on OneMotoring’s buying pages). Renewal means paying PQP under the renewal rules. This guide does not recommend either path — it only separates the two prices so you do not paste the wrong number into a spreadsheet.

Off-peak and weekend car schemes

OneMotoring states that Weekend Car / Off-Peak Car / Revised Off-Peak Car renewals pay the same PQP rate according to the vehicle’s category, and the vehicle remains under its scheme after renewal. Check the live OPC/ROPC pages on OneMotoring for usage-hour rules; those are separate from PQP math. Source: OneMotoring COE Renewal.

Bottom line: PQP is a three-month moving average used for renewal, published monthly after the previous month’s second bidding. Latest bidding QP is related noise, not the same number. Always confirm the payable PQP on OneMotoring before you transfer funds.
