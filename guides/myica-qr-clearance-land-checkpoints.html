MyICA QR Code Clearance at Woodlands and Tuas Checkpoints

How to generate a MyICA QR code for faster immigration clearance at Woodlands and Tuas, who is eligible, and what to prepare before year-end school-holiday crossings.

Updated 6 November 2026
WhatsApp

If you cross Woodlands Checkpoint or Tuas Checkpoint by road, the Immigration & Checkpoints Authority (ICA) wants you to clear with a QR code from the MyICA mobile app whenever you can. It does not replace your passport for travel, and it does not remove the need to check traffic before you leave — but it is one of the few steps you can finish at home that speeds up Singapore-side clearance once you reach the booth.

This guide summarises ICA’s published QR rules for land checkpoints (page last updated 27 October 2026) and pairs them with live camera checks on SG Alerts. Always prefer ICA’s own page if anything here and the official wording differ.

Year-end school holidays framing (2026–27)

MOE Term 4 holidays for MK / Primary / Secondary run 21 November–31 December 2026 (post-secondary Term 4 holidays on the MOE calendar: 28 November–31 December 2026). Christmas Day is Friday, 25 December 2026; New Year’s Day is Friday, 1 January 2027 (MOM). Generate or refresh MyICA QR codes before those busy travel days — not in the approach queue.

What MyICA QR clearance is (and is not)

ICA’s page on QR code immigration clearance at land checkpoints explains that you generate a QR code in MyICA and self-scan it at Woodlands or Tuas when arriving or departing by car, motorcycle, lorry, or bus.

You can create an individual QR code or a group QR code if you are travelling with others. At the lane, you scan the QR instead of handing over your passport as the first step — but ICA still says you must bring a valid passport, and officers may still ask to see it for verification.

Important limits from ICA:

  • The QR is for Singapore land-checkpoint clearance only. It is not a substitute for Malaysian immigration, tolls, or other roadside checks.
  • You still need your passport as your travel document when going overseas.
  • QR codes can be generated and retrieved offline after you create them, so a dead mobile-data signal at the booth should not block a code you already generated. ICA still advises generating the code before you reach the checkpoint.

Who can use it

According to ICA, Singapore Citizens, Permanent Residents, Long-Term Pass holders with a Foreign Identification Number (FIN), and foreign visitors can use QR clearance for arrival or departure by car, motorcycle, lorry, or bus — with two clear exceptions:

  • You cannot use a QR code if you are entering Singapore as a first-time foreign visitor.
  • You cannot use a QR code if you are re-entering Singapore on a different passport from your last visit. If the passport has never been used for entry into Singapore before, ICA says you must present the passport for clearance; QR clearance is available on subsequent trips after that passport has been used.

For automated immigration lanes, ICA also notes that your biometric identifiers must already be enrolled with ICA or MOM. See ICA’s materials on automated lanes if you use passenger halls rather than a vehicle booth.

To generate a code you need a phone with the MyICA app, plus your passport or Singpass (for SCs, PRs, and Long-Term Pass holders), as listed on ICA’s QR page.

What to do before you leave home

  1. Update MyICA from the App Store or Google Play. Keep the app current even if you are a citizen who mainly uses QR (short-term visitors also use MyICA for SG Arrival Card flows).
  2. Create or refresh your profile in MyICA and generate an individual or group QR code for everyone travelling in your vehicle or party, following the in-app steps ICA documents.
  3. Confirm eligibility for each person (especially first-time foreign visitors and anyone on a newly issued passport).
  4. Keep passports accessible even though you plan to scan a QR. ICA may still ask for the booklet.
  5. Check live cameras so you are not “QR-ready” but stuck in an approach queue you could have avoided. Use Woodlands traffic cameras and Tuas traffic cameras, and compare crossings in Woodlands vs Tuas.

At the checkpoint

ICA’s procedure is straightforward: before you reach the booth, have the QR ready; at the lane, self-scan it instead of presenting your passport as the default first step. If you accidentally scan a QR that does not match your particulars, ICA says the system or officers will guide you — but that can take longer than a clean scan.

Stay in the correct vehicle lane, follow marshals, and do not cut queues. ICA’s land-checkpoint advisories have warned that queue-cutting can result in being sent to the back of the queue.

QR clearance helps the immigration step. It does not shorten Malaysian-side processing, Second Link or Causeway road congestion, or fuel/VEP checks. Treat it as one part of a calm crossing routine, not a guarantee of a short trip.

Past ICA note: Deepavali long weekend (6–9 November 2026)

ICA’s advisory for the Deepavali long weekend of 6–9 November 2026 encouraged MyICA QR use and stated that initiatives such as QR code clearance and facial recognition for motorcyclists and pillion riders had increased clearance throughput by more than 35 per cent during departure peak periods compared with pre-COVID levels in 2019. That figure is ICA’s published claim for those initiatives as a group — not a personal wait-time promise for your next trip. That Deepavali window is now past; keep the QR habit for the coming school holidays. Source: ICA Deepavali land-checkpoint advisory.

Pair QR prep with a traffic check

A prepared QR code does not tell you which checkpoint is clearer tonight. Before you commit to Woodlands or Tuas:

  • Open the Woodlands cameras and note the image timestamps.
  • Open the Tuas cameras and do the same.
  • Factor in where you are starting from in Singapore and where you are going in Johor — see Woodlands vs Tuas.
  • For year-end calendar windows, see year-end Causeway timing; for petrol / VEP, see JB tips.

Quick checklist

  • MyICA installed and updated
  • Individual or group QR generated before you leave
  • Passports packed and valid
  • Everyone in the vehicle eligible for QR (watch first-time visitors and new passports)
  • Woodlands and Tuas cameras checked with fresh timestamps
  • ICA year-end advisory: TBC — re-check media room before major holiday departures
  • Backup plan if your preferred checkpoint suddenly queues

Bottom line: Generate the MyICA QR at home, bring your passport anyway, and verify lane conditions on the live cameras before you roll out. Official detail always lives on ICA’s QR page linked above.
