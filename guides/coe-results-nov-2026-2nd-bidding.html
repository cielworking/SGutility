COE Results Explained: November 2026 2nd Bidding (16–18 Nov)

How to read LTA COE results for the 16–18 November 2026 exercise: categories, Quota Premium vs PQP, release timing after 4pm close, and next bidding dates.

Updated 6 November 2026
WhatsApp

Every COE open bidding exercise ends the same way for most Singapore buyers: a short results table, a few big numbers, and a lot of group-chat noise. This guide is written around the 16–18 November 2026 exercise, but it is meant to stay useful after every round — swap in the new Quota Premiums when LTA publishes them, keep the category definitions, and move the calendar forward.

Official schedule and live results live on LTA OneMotoring. SG Alerts mirrors published premiums on latest COE prices. If figures ever disagree, trust OneMotoring.

This round’s bidding window

LTA’s 2026 COE bidding schedule lists the November 2026 second exercise as:

  • Opens: Monday, 16 November 2026, 12 noon
  • Closes: Wednesday, 18 November 2026, 4pm

Source PDF: COE Open Bidding Exercises schedule for 2026. OneMotoring’s COE page also states that exercises usually start at 12pm on the first and third Monday of the month and end at 4pm on Wednesday if there is no public holiday in between — Certificate of Entitlement (COE).

For a fuller date list through January 2027, see When is the next COE bidding?.

What the categories mean

OneMotoring’s open-bidding results page explains Categories A and B (rules from the May 2022 first exercise onwards):

  • Category A: non-fully electric cars with engines up to 1,600cc and maximum power output up to 97kW (130bhp); and fully electric cars with maximum power output up to 110kW (147bhp).
  • Category B: non-fully electric cars with engines above 1,600cc or maximum power output above 97kW (130bhp); and fully electric cars with maximum power output above 110kW (147bhp).
  • Category C: goods vehicles and buses.
  • Category D: motorcycles.
  • Category E: open (all except motorcycle).

Source: COE Open Bidding. For a buyer-focused walkthrough of A vs B, see COE Cat A vs Cat B explained.

Quota Premium (QP) vs Prevailing Quota Premium (PQP)

Quota Premium (QP) is the clearing price successful bidders pay for that category in that exercise. OneMotoring shows QP alongside the quota and bid counts after each round.

Prevailing Quota Premium (PQP) is different. LTA’s COE page describes PQP as the moving average of COE prices in the last three months, used when you renew an existing vehicle’s COE without bidding again. Do not treat the latest QP as your renewal PQP — check the PQP figures LTA publishes for renewals.

Source: OneMotoring COE page.

How to read a results table

  • Quota: how many COEs were available in that category for the exercise (unused quota can carry forward under LTA’s rules).
  • QP ($): the price successful bidders in that category pay for this round.
  • Received / successful / unsuccessful: bid volume and outcomes — useful for sensing demand, not a promise about next round.
  • Change vs previous exercise: SG Alerts shows the movement on latest COE prices once the new QP is published.

Most private buyers never bid personally; dealers bid as part of a package. Ask whether your package is guaranteed, what happens if the bid fails, and whether the quoted car price already builds in a COE buffer.

During the exercise (for those who bid)

If you or your dealer are in the exercise, OneMotoring provides an Enquire/Revise COE Bid digital service (Singpass login) so you can see whether your bid status is still accepted or has been out-bid as the Current COE Price moves. Individuals typically bid via participating bank channels (OneMotoring documents ATM/internet banking options and deposits on its COE pages). Keep your acknowledgement details safe for revisions.

Remember the uniform-price rule described on LTA materials and in our Cat A vs Cat B guide: successful bidders in a category pay the same Quota Premium for that exercise, not each person’s maximum reserve. Bidding higher than the eventual QP improves your chance of staying in; it does not make you pay more than other winners in that category.

After a successful bid, OneMotoring explains Temporary COE (TCOE) validity periods by category — register the vehicle before the TCOE expires or risk forfeiting the deposit under LTA’s rules. Unsuccessful deposits are refunded after fees according to LTA’s published process (OneMotoring notes refund timing relative to results announcement).

When are results released?

Each exercise closes at 4pm on the end date in LTA’s schedule. OneMotoring’s open-bidding page then shows that the exercise has ended and publishes the results table. LTA’s COE page says you will know if your bid is successful when the bidding results are announced.

What we do not invent: LTA does not publish a fixed “results always appear at 4:0X pm” minute in the materials checked for this guide. Practical habit: from 4pm on closing day, refresh OneMotoring COE Open Bidding, then check SG Alerts.
