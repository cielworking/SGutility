Cross-Border Motoring Rules

Singapore 3/4-Tank Rule & JB Petrol Guide: Fines, Hybrid/EV Rules & RON 97

Every Singapore-registered petrol, diesel, CNG, or hybrid car departing Singapore via Woodlands Checkpoint or Tuas Checkpoint must have its fuel gauge at or above the three-quarter (3/4) mark. Understanding the exact rules saves you from a costly $500 fine and being turned around in peak traffic.

Which Vehicles Must Comply With the 3/4-Tank Rule?

Vehicle Type (SG-Registered)3/4-Tank Rule Applies?Notes
Petrol & Diesel CarsYes (Mandatory)Fuel needle / digital bar must read at least 3/4 full at departure
Hybrid & Plug-In Hybrid (PHEV)Yes (Mandatory)Applies to the petrol tank regardless of battery state of charge
CNG / Bi-Fuel VehiclesYes (Mandatory)Applies to the motor fuel supply tank
Fully Electric Vehicles (BEV)No (Exempt)No combustion fuel tank; ensure sufficient battery charge for Causeway jams
MotorcyclesNo (Exempt)Singapore-registered motorcycles are exempt from the 3/4-tank rule
Foreign-Registered CarsNo (Exempt)Rule only applies to Singapore-registered vehicles leaving Singapore

Penalties & Enforcement at Woodlands and Tuas

  • Composition Fine up to $500: Singapore Customs enforces the rule under the Customs Act via random checks at land checkpoint departure lanes. Drivers who breach the requirement face a composition sum of up to $500 or prosecution in court.
  • Mandatory U-Turn: Even after receiving a fine, non-compliant vehicles are turned back into Singapore to refuel — costing you an extra 45–90 minutes if Causeway traffic is heavy.
  • Tampered Fuel Gauges: Deliberately altering a fuel gauge carries severe court penalties, including higher fines and potential prosecution.

Malaysia Petrol Rules: RON 97 Only for Foreign Cars

Once in Johor Bahru, remember that Malaysian law restricts foreign-registered vehicles (including Singapore-plated cars) from pumping subsidised RON 95 (yellow nozzle). You may only pump unsubsidised RON 97 (green nozzle) or higher premium grades (e.g., RON 100 / Shell V-Power Racing).

Singapore 3/4-tank rule FAQs

Does the Singapore 3/4-tank rule apply to hybrid cars and electric vehicles (EVs)?
Petrol-electric hybrid cars and plug-in hybrids with a fuel tank MUST comply with the three-quarter tank rule. Fully electric vehicles (BEVs) with no fuel tank are exempt because they do not run on petrol, diesel, or CNG.
Do Singapore motorcycles have to follow the 3/4-tank rule?
No. The Singapore Customs three-quarter tank rule applies to Singapore-registered motor vehicles with four or more wheels (cars, vans, lorries, buses) running on petrol, diesel, or CNG. Motorcycles are exempt.
Can Singapore-registered cars pump RON 95 petrol in Johor Bahru?
No. Malaysian regulations prohibit foreign-registered vehicles (including Singapore-plated cars) from purchasing subsidised RON 95 petrol. Singapore drivers may only purchase unsubsidised RON 97 or premium grades (such as RON 100) in Malaysia.
