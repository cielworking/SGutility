How to Check ERP Rates in Singapore

Use OneMotoring’s official map and rate PDFs for your gantries and vehicle type — and know when ERP does not run.

Updated 6 November 2026

ERP rates change by gantry, time slot and vehicle type — and LTA reviews them regularly. Memorising a screenshot from a group chat is a weak plan. This guide shows how to check the official rate for your trip, when ERP usually runs, and how that fits next to petrol and checkpoint planning on SG Alerts. It does not republish a full gantry table (those PDFs change).

What ERP is charging for

Electronic Road Pricing is Singapore’s congestion charge. OneMotoring’s ERP page states that you pay a fee when you drive through an ERP gantry during operational hours. Rates are reviewed regularly so traffic can move at an optimal speed. Charge depends on vehicle type (larger vehicle types pay more) and the time you enter the gantry; during peaks, charges can change every half hour. Source: OneMotoring — Electronic Road Pricing (ERP).

LTA also explains how time slots work: a period written as 0730–0800 means the rate applies from and including 7.30am until but excluding 8.00am. The same rule applies to other slots. Source: same OneMotoring ERP page.

When ERP runs (and when it does not)

OneMotoring currently states that ERP is operational from Mondays to Fridays and is not in operation on Saturdays, Sundays or public holidays. Source: OneMotoring ERP.

On the eve of selected major public holidays, ERP stops earlier — OneMotoring says operations end at 1:00 pm. The same page lists slightly different holiday names in two places (one note mentions Hari Raya Haji; a later section mentions Hari Raya Puasa, alongside New Year’s Day, Chinese New Year, Deepavali and Christmas). Treat the eve list as something to re-check on OneMotoring before you drive, rather than a fact frozen in this article. Weekend and full public-holiday “no ERP” wording is stated clearly in both places.

Rates are reviewed every quarter and adjusted during the June and December school holidays based on traffic conditions at the time. Source: OneMotoring ERP page. LTA also publishes news releases when cordons or rates change — for example, the 7 September 2026 revision that resumed weekday Orchard Cordon ERP for stated time slots. Source: LTA news release — Revised ERP Rates from 7 September 2026. Always prefer the live rate table or interactive map over an old news paragraph.

How to check the rate for your route

  1. Open the official OneMotoring ERP page.
  2. Use the interactive map / rate checker on that page (or linked traffic tools) for the gantries you will pass, at the time you expect to pass them, for your vehicle type.
  3. If you prefer a printable source of truth, download the current ERP rates PDF from the same page’s “Rates for download” section. OneMotoring lists multiple effective dates — pick the PDF whose “with effect from” date covers your travel day.
  4. Add up only the gantries you will actually enter during operational hours. A free slot on the table means no charge for that period.

Do not copy dollar amounts from third-party blogs into your budget without verifying against LTA’s table. Third-party tools can lag a quarterly revision.

Payment basics (so a rate check is not wasted)

Singapore-registered vehicles need an In-vehicle Unit (IU) or On-Board Unit (OBU) to drive through operating ERP gantries. Payment is typically via a stored-value card in the IU, or via auto top-up / backend payment services described on OneMotoring. From 1 October 2024, LTA describes a five-day SMS grace period for missed gantry payments before an administrative charge applies — details and channels are on the ERP page. Source: OneMotoring ERP.

Foreign-registered vehicles follow different payment rules by vehicle type (including flat-rate options for some passenger cars). If you are bringing a Malaysia-registered car into Singapore, read the foreign-vehicle section on OneMotoring rather than assuming the same card flow as a Singapore IU. For checkpoint planning itself, use Woodlands cameras / Tuas cameras and the land-crossing guides — ERP is a separate on-island cost.

How this fits SG Alerts tools

  • Petrol: ERP is another variable driving cost next to pump prices. Check Singapore petrol prices when you are comparing “drive vs don’t” for a weekday CBD trip; use the how to check petrol prices guide for board-price habits.
  • Public holidays / weekends: No ERP on Sat/Sun/PH (per OneMotoring) can change the “is driving worth it?” math even when petrol is high — but holiday roads can still be congested. Pair with 2027 long-weekend planning when published.
  • Checkpoints: ERP does not replace VEP, tank or Autopass rules for JB trips. Keep using the Causeway / Second Link camera pages before you leave.

ERP 2.0 transition note

OneMotoring’s ERP page notes that LTA is transitioning to ERP 2.0 and that IU use will be phased out by end 2026, with foreign-registered vehicle rules changing around OBU installation dates stated on that page. Treat those dates and flat fees as live OneMotoring facts — re-read before a Malaysia-registered car trip rather than relying on this paragraph. Source: OneMotoring ERP.

A short pre-drive checklist

  • Day type: weekday vs weekend/public holiday (ERP off on Sat/Sun/PH per OneMotoring)
  • If eve of a major holiday: confirm whether ERP ends at 1pm on OneMotoring
  • Gantry list + time slots checked on official map or PDF for your vehicle type
  • IU/OBU and payment method ready (or foreign-vehicle rule understood)
  • Petrol plan checked on SG Alerts petrol if fuel cost matters for the same trip

Bottom line: Treat ERP like petrol — look up the official figure for this journey instead of recycling last month’s chat screenshot. OneMotoring is the source of truth; SG Alerts helps you stack that check next to petrol and checkpoint cams.
