How to Check Singapore Petrol Prices Today

How to read Singapore pump (board) prices by grade, use timestamped comparison tables, and confirm at the pump — separate from card-discount stacking.

Updated 6 November 2026

Singapore drivers search “petrol price today” for a simple reason: board prices move, and the cheapest sticker is not always obvious across brands and grades. This guide is about how to check and read listed pump prices — where to look, which grade column matters, and how to use timestamps — not about stacking every loyalty card.

For membership discounts, bank cards and effective-price maths, use the separate guide How to pay less for petrol in Singapore. Keep the two jobs separate so you do not confuse a board price with a nett price.

What “petrol price” usually means in Singapore searches

Most comparison tables show the published pump (board) price for each brand and grade: commonly 92, 95, 98, premium variants, and diesel. That is the starting number before membership or card discounts. Retailers can change prices without a fixed public timetable, so “today” only means “as of the timestamp on the source you are reading.”

SG Alerts maintains a live comparison on Singapore petrol prices, covering major brands with source timestamps and notes on savings where available. Always confirm at the pump before you fill — dashboards can lag a price change by minutes or hours depending on when the source was checked.

A practical check routine

  1. Open one trusted comparison — for example SG Alerts petrol prices — and note the “last updated” / source-checked time.
  2. Pick the grade you actually use (most cars run 95 or 98; diesel drivers must ignore petrol columns entirely).
  3. Scan that column across brands, including stations that show N/A for grades they do not sell.
  4. Look for “cheapest” highlights if the table marks them — treat that as a pointer to the lowest listed board price in that grade at scrape time, not a promise after discounts.
  5. Decide whether a detour is worth it before you drive across the island for a one-cent board gap.
  6. If you care about nett cost, only then open the pay-less guide and your fuel app / bank promo terms.

How to read a comparison table without fooling yourself

  • Brand rows vs grade columns: the intersection is the board price. Empty or N/A cells mean that brand is not offering that grade in the feed — not that petrol is free.
  • Premium / specialty fuels: higher board prices are normal; do not average them into “cheapest 95.”
  • Diesel: compare diesel only to diesel. A station that is cheapest on 95 may not win on diesel.
  • Timestamps beat screenshots: a friend’s photo from this morning can already be wrong. Prefer a page that states when it last checked official or retailer sources.
  • Pump is final: if the pole price differs from the website, believe the pole (and the receipt).

Official and primary sources (when you want to verify)

Unlike COE or PSI, there is no single government “daily petrol price” page that lists every brand’s board price in one official table for consumers. Prices are set by oil companies. Practical verification paths:

  • Retailer apps and station sites (Shell, Esso, SPC, Caltex, Sinopec, and others operating in Singapore).
  • Independent dashboards that cite update times — including SG Alerts.
  • Your own eyes at the pump before you authorise payment.

If you are filling before a Malaysia trip, remember Singapore’s three-quarter tank exit rule and Malaysia’s RON95 restrictions for foreign-registered cars are separate topics — covered in school-holiday JB petrol & Causeway tips.

Mobile vs desktop reading tips

On a phone, petrol tables often scroll sideways. Pin the grade column you care about mentally (or screenshot only after checking the timestamp). Full-width “Refresh” controls on SG Alerts reload the latest saved copy — useful before a commute fill-up, not a substitute for reading the pole.

Brand logos and “CHEAPEST” badges, when shown, are visual shortcuts. They still refer to listed board prices in that grade at update time. After any membership or credit-card cut, the winner can change — which is why discount stacking lives in the pay-less guide.

When prices disagree between sites

Different dashboards scrape or update on different clocks. If Site A and Site B disagree by a few cents, check timestamps first, then the oil company’s own app, then the station pole. Do not average conflicting figures into a DIY “official” price. There is no single government bulletin that settles every brand’s board price each morning in the sources reviewed for this guide.

What this page will not invent

  • No claimed “average Singapore petrol price” for the week without a cited dataset.
  • No promise that a dashboard’s “cheapest” cell survives your card discount stack.
  • No fixed hour of day when all brands cut prices — retailers do not publish a shared schedule in the sources checked for this guide.

Linking board price to a fill-up decision

Once you know today’s lowest board price for your grade, ask only two follow-ups: is the station on your route, and will any membership you already have change the ranking? If you do not have a membership yet, finish the fill-up first using the board comparison — joining three apps at the pump gantry is how people miss a cheaper pole two roads away.

Quick checklist before you drive to the station

  • Grade selected (92 / 95 / 98 / diesel)
  • Comparison page timestamp is fresh enough for your trip
  • Lowest board price noted — and distance cost considered
  • Membership / card plan checked only if you are optimising nett price (pay-less guide)
  • Pole price confirmed before pumping

Bottom line: Checking petrol prices means reading board prices by grade with a trustworthy timestamp, then confirming at the pump. Saving money on top of that is a second step — link out to the pay-less guide instead of mixing both jobs here.
